03 — How we work

No surprises, start to snagging

Most complaints about builders come down to the same three things: vague prices, silent phones and unfinished last jobs. Here is exactly how a job with us runs.

Our five promises

  • You get a named contact — Ewan — for the whole job.
  • The price on the quote is the price on the invoice, unless you change the scope in writing.
  • Dust screens up, floors protected, site swept every evening.
  • We finish the job before we start the next one.
  • Calls and emails answered within one working day.
  1. 01

    Site visit and measure

    A proper hour on site, usually within a week of you getting in touch. We measure, take levels, look under the floor and tell you honestly what the job needs.

    Free · evenings available

  2. 02

    Drawings and fixed quote

    You receive a written quote broken down by stage, with materials specified by name, plus setting-out drawings for kitchens and joinery so you can see it before it is built.

    Within 5 working days

  3. 03

    Warrants and programme

    Where a building warrant is needed we prepare and submit the application, then give you a dated programme showing what happens in which week.

    Typically 8–12 weeks for approval

  4. 04

    On the tools

    We start when we said we would and work 7.30am to 5pm. You get a short update at the end of each week and a call before anything changes.

    One job at a time

  5. 05

    Snagging and sign-off

    We walk the job with you and write the snag list ourselves. Everything on it is cleared before the final invoice is issued, not after.

    Completion certificate where applicable

  6. 06

    Guarantee and aftercare

    Ten years on our workmanship, in writing, plus the manufacturer paperwork for everything we installed. Doors ease and timber moves — ring us and we will come back.

    10-year written guarantee

05 — Questions

The things everybody asks

If your question is not here, put it in the form below or ring the workshop. We would rather answer it now than on week three.

How far ahead are you booking?

Kitchens and joinery are currently going in six to eight weeks ahead. Extensions and loft conversions need three to four months, mostly because of warrant approval rather than our diary. Small repair work we can often slot in within a fortnight.

Do I need a building warrant?

Almost all extensions, loft conversions and structural alterations do, and so does anything that changes a load-bearing wall. Straight kitchen replacements and joinery usually do not. We will tell you at the site visit, and if a warrant is needed we prepare the drawings and submit the application to your local authority on your behalf.

How do you price a job?

Fixed price, written down, broken into stages. We do not work on day rates for planned jobs. The quote lists the materials by manufacturer and specification, so if you compare us against someone else you are comparing like for like. Quotes are free within thirty miles of Dollar.

Do you want a deposit up front?

We never ask for money for labour before we start. On larger jobs there is a materials payment once the order is placed — kitchen units, steels, windows — and then staged payments as each stage completes. Final payment is due after the snag list is signed off, not before.

Can I stay in the house while you work?

In nearly every case, yes. Kitchens usually mean about a week on a temporary sink and a microwave in the dining room, and we will help you set that up. Extensions are worked from the outside for as long as possible, and the wall only comes through near the end.

Are you insured, and can I see it?

Yes — £5m public liability and £10m employers' liability. Ask and we will email the certificates with the quote, along with our waste carrier registration and the guarantee wording.

What happens if something goes wrong later?

Ring us. Our workmanship guarantee runs for ten years and it is a real document, not a line on a website. Timber moves and doors need easing after a winter — that is normal, and we do not charge to come back and sort it.
