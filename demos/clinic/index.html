Your first visit

Fifty minutes, three parts, and a written plan at the end of it.

0 min elapsed

  1. 0–15 min

    Assessment

    A full history and a proper look at how you move, not just where it hurts. What happened, when it started, what makes it worse, what you have already tried, and what you are trying to get back to.

  2. 15–40 min

    Hands-on treatment

    Joint mobilisation, soft tissue work or dry needling, whichever fits what is found. We explain each test before doing it and stop the moment you want to. You will not be asked to come back a second time simply to start treatment.

  3. 40–50 min

    Your plan

    A written, graded exercise programme by email the same evening, usually three or four exercises rather than fifteen, and a straight answer on what to expect and what to do if it flares.

Fifty minutes for a first appointment, thirty for a follow-up, and one person in the room. Nobody is treated on two plinths at once.

Treatments

What we do, and what each one is for

Everything happens in one quiet treatment room. Most people need somewhere between three and six appointments, and we will tell you honestly at the end of the first one.

Physiotherapy

For pain that has a cause worth finding.

A full musculoskeletal assessment, hands-on treatment (joint mobilisation, soft tissue work, taping where it helps) and a graded exercise programme. Best for back and neck pain, joint problems, and anything that has not settled on its own after a fortnight.

Sports massage

For tight, overworked, heavy-feeling muscles.

Deep soft tissue work for training loads, long shifts on your feet or a stiff back after a week at a desk. Useful in a race build-up and just as useful in week three of a garden project.

Rehabilitation

For getting strong enough that it stays fixed.

Structured, progressive loading after an injury or an operation: knee and shoulder surgery, ankle sprains, hamstring tears, tendon problems. Written programmes with sets, reps and a date to review them.

Dry needling

For stubborn trigger points that will not let go.

Fine acupuncture needles into a tight band of muscle to settle it down. Carried out by an AACP-accredited physiotherapist, always with your consent, and always as part of a wider plan rather than a treatment on its own.

Injury assessment and second opinion

For when you want to know what you are dealing with.

A one-off appointment: history, movement testing, clinical reasoning and a clear written summary you can take to your GP, consultant, coach or insurer. If you need a scan or a referral, we will say so plainly rather than book you in for six weeks.

Things people ask before booking

Do I need a referral from my GP?

No. Physiotherapy in Scotland is a direct-access profession, so you can book yourself in. If we think your GP ought to be involved, for a scan, medication or an onward referral, we will write to them with your permission.

How many appointments will I need?

Most problems settle in three to six appointments over four to eight weeks. Some need one. Long-standing back pain and post-operative rehabilitation take longer, and we will say so on day one rather than at appointment number seven.

Will it hurt?

Assessment can reproduce your symptoms briefly, because that is often how we identify the source. Treatment should be comfortable, firm at times, but never something you have to grit your teeth through. You can stop anything at any point, and it is normal to feel a little achy for a day afterwards.

What should I wear, and how do I pay?

Loose, comfortable clothes you can move in. Shorts are handy for knee, hip and ankle problems; a vest top for shoulders and necks. Spares are available if you have come straight from work.

Card, cash or bank transfer at the end of the appointment, and no deposit to book. You are welcome to bring a friend, partner or chaperone into the room. Just let us know when you book.

Is dry needling the same as acupuncture?

Similar needles, different reasoning. Traditional acupuncture follows meridian points; dry needling targets a specific tight band of muscle to release it. It is done by an AACP-accredited physiotherapist using single-use sterile needles, and it is always optional. Plenty of people decline and do perfectly well without.

I am not sporty at all. Is this clinic for me?

Very much so. On a typical day we see more office workers, nurses, tradespeople, gardeners and retired walkers than athletes. “Sports injury” simply describes the kind of injury, not the kind of person.

Can I claim it on my private health insurance?

We are not currently registered with the insurers directly, so you pay us at the appointment and we issue a full itemised invoice with our registration details for you to self-claim. Most policies accept this, and it is worth ringing your insurer first to confirm.

What if I need to cancel?

Let us know at least twenty-four hours ahead and there is no charge. Under that, we charge half the appointment fee, because the slot usually cannot be filled. Illness and genuine emergencies are always waived. Just tell us.

Do you treat children?

We see young people from twelve upwards for sports injuries, accompanied by a parent or guardian for the whole appointment. For younger children we would point you towards the NHS paediatric physiotherapy service, which is better set up for it.
