Privacy notice

Last updated: 1 October 2026. Logan Tempany, trading as Tempany Web Studios in Dollar, Scotland, is responsible for the personal information collected through this website. Questions can be sent to logan@tempanywebstudios.co.uk.

The reply form collects your name, business name, email address, current website if supplied, what your enquiry is about, the details you enter in your message, and the trade and business name you pencilled on the sketchpad, if you did. This information is used to review and reply to your enquiry, prepare a quotation and, if you choose to proceed, arrange and deliver the requested work. It is processed to take steps at your request before a possible contract, to manage genuine business enquiries, and to perform any agreement you enter into. If you arrived by clicking one of my Google adverts and send the form from the page the advert opened, the message also carries one line saying “Google advert”; otherwise it says “Not marked as from an advert”. That line is worked out from the page address when you press send. Nothing is stored on your device for it, it is not sent to Google Analytics or Google Ads, and it is used only to see which adverts bring enquiries, on the basis of legitimate interests (UK GDPR Article 6(1)(f)).

The form uses Web3Forms to deliver submissions to the business inbox without opening your email app. Data is transmitted over HTTPS. Web3Forms states that submission data may be retained for a maximum of three years, or for a shorter period where the plan provides one or it is deleted earlier, and may be processed outside the UK under the safeguards described in its policy. Google processes the delivered email as the inbox provider. If JavaScript is switched off, the form is posted to Web3Forms directly, and Web3Forms returns you to a thank-you page on this site.

Enquiries that do not become projects are normally deleted from the business inbox within 12 months. If work proceeds, relevant communications may be kept for longer where needed to deliver the project, maintain financial records, enforce an agreement or resolve a dispute. Information is not sold or used for unrelated marketing.

You may ask for access to your personal information, request correction or deletion where applicable, or restrict or object to certain processing. You may also make a data protection complaint using the process below.

Preview pages

Some pages on this website are previews: first versions of websites made for particular businesses, under /concepts/, and example websites, under /demos/. When one of these pages is opened, Logan receives a short notification on his phone, so that he can follow up while the preview is fresh and does not chase anyone who has not seen it.

The notification says which page was opened and when; the approximate town, region and country, and the name of the internet provider, which Cloudflare works out from the connection's IP address; the type of device and browser; and the website the link was followed from, where the browser passes that on. A link Logan sends to a business can end in a short label naming that business, such as ?for=business-name, and the notification shows that label too. On mobile data the town is often the network's nearest hub rather than where you are. The IP address itself is not included and is not stored. So that one visit sends one notification, a one-way code made from the IP address, the browser and the preview is held in Cloudflare's cache for up to 30 minutes and then discarded; the address cannot be worked out from it. Nothing is stored on your device.

Notifications are delivered through Telegram, which states that data for users who signed up from the UK or the EEA is stored in data centres in the Netherlands. Logan keeps them for no longer than one month and uses them only to decide when to follow up. They are not combined with other information, used for advertising or shared.

This is done on the basis of legitimate interests: previews are made for, and sent to, businesses Logan would like to work with, and knowing whether one has been opened lets him follow up at a sensible time, or not at all. To stop notifications about your visits from a browser, open tempanywebstudios.co.uk/quiet/ in it. You can also ask for any notification about your visit to be deleted by emailing the address above.

Cookies

Nothing from Google is stored on your device or read from it unless you say yes. A slip asks, separately for Google Analytics and for Google Ads. Until you answer, and if you refuse, Google's tag is not loaded and nothing is sent to Google.

Google Analytics, if you allow it, counts visits to the main pages. It records which pages are opened, with the full address you arrived at, campaign tags included (such as utm_source=google), and the site or page you came from; whether pages are scrolled to the end; which links out of the site are followed; the kind of device and browser; and roughly where you are, which Google works out from the connection's IP address without keeping the address. Unless you also allow Google Ads, advert click identifiers (gclid, gbraid, wbraid, dclid and gclsrc) are taken out of those addresses before they reach Google. When a form is sent, it also records that an enquiry was made, carrying only the form's name (home, audit or stirling) and nothing you typed. Google signals are switched off, and nothing is used for personalised advertising. Google processes the data under its data processing terms. Two of Google's sharing settings are on: technical support, so Google's support staff can look at the account if something goes wrong, and modelling contributions, which Google says are aggregated with data from other websites and apps so that they cannot identify you. Sharing with other Google products and services is off. The data is kept for 14 months; after that only aggregated totals remain.

Google Ads, if you allow it, lets Google Ads link a click on one of my Google adverts to your visit and to an enquiry sent from the page, so I can see which adverts bring enquiries. Google also uses this for its own purposes, such as measuring advertising and protecting against fraud, as it explains at policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites. Personalised advertising stays off whatever you choose.

When you refuse something you had allowed, the Google cookies this site set for it are deleted at once and nothing new is recorded for it, though anything recorded in the few seconds before may still be sent. If you refuse Google Analytics after allowing it, your browser first reads the random ID Google Analytics gave it and sends it to me through Web3Forms, the form service above, in a message holding only that ID and the date. Within one month I delete the visits Google Analytics holds under that ID; Google says they stop showing within 24 hours and are permanently removed within the following 63 days. What Google Ads has already received stays with Google, which says it anonymises advertising data in its server logs by removing part of the IP address after 9 months and cookie information after 18 months, as its retention page explains.

If your browser sends Global Privacy Control, it is taken as a refusal: Google's tag never loads, you are not asked, and any Google cookies this site set earlier are deleted. If you had allowed Google Analytics before your browser started sending it, that counts as refusing it after allowing it: your browser sends me its Google Analytics ID once, as above, and I delete those visits. Any earlier yes is replaced by that refusal, kept like an answer on the slip, so Google stays off if your browser later stops sending Global Privacy Control. With JavaScript switched off, nothing is asked and the tag cannot load.

Google may process this data in the United States. For transfers from the UK it relies on the UK Extension to the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework, under which Google LLC is certified, and otherwise on standard contractual clauses. Google explains this at business.safety.google/adsdatatransfers and in its privacy policy.

Three things are kept in your browser without asking, because each only remembers something you did:

  • tws-consent: your answer on the slip, or a refusal taken from Global Privacy Control, and its date, so you are not asked on every page. You are asked again after 6 months.
  • tws-theme: light or dark, only if you press the switch.
  • tws_quiet: a cookie, only if you open /quiet/, kept for up to 400 days so that opening a preview page from that browser does not send a notification; /quiet/?undo removes it.

The preview pages keep their own light or dark setting and cookie choice in the same way, and two of them remember what you ticked or typed on the page until the tab is closed; nothing is stored until you press something. Visits to the main pages are counted with Cloudflare Web Analytics, which does not use cookies, does not fingerprint the device and does not follow you to other sites. Neither it nor Google's tag runs on the preview pages.
