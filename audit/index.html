Most website audits are a free PDF from a scanner with a sales call attached. This one is me, sitting with your site on a phone and a laptop for a couple of hours, writing down what's costing you enquiries in the order it's costing you. Sometimes the answer is a rebuild. Request the audit

Who it's for. Anyone with a site that was built a while ago, by a friend, by a previous agency or by yourself on Wix or Squarespace, who isn't sure whether it's working and doesn't want to be sold a rebuild to find out.

Who it isn't for. A business with no site yet. If that's you, the £399 quote is the right page, and the first screen of your homepage is roughed out free before you commit to anything.

What's in it

The same things a customer meets, checked in the order they meet them. Nothing here needs you to understand the technical side; the report translates it.

01

What I look at

  • On a phone, first. Whether it loads, whether the text reads without pinching, whether your phone number is a button or just a number.
  • What Google is shown. The page title and description, whether the site is indexed at all, and whether your Business Profile and your site agree on your name, number and town.
  • The route to an enquiry. How many taps from landing to calling you or sending a form, whether the form actually works, and where it goes.
  • Speed and the boring fatal things. How long the first screen takes on 4G, an expired certificate, a copyright year that stopped, links that go nowhere.
  • Who owns what. Whether the domain, the hosting and the site itself are in your name or in someone else's.
02

What you get

A written report in plain English, usually two pages. Every finding is ranked by what it costs you in enquiries, not by how technical it sounds, and each one comes with the fix, the effort, and a straight recommendation: fix it, rebuild, or leave it alone.

No severity tables, no scanner output, no screenshots of a dashboard. If something is fine, the report says it's fine.

Questions you might have

Yes. I audit the site, not the tool it was built with. Most of what costs a small business enquiries has nothing to do with the platform.

Often. That's the point of doing this before quoting anything. The report tells you which, and why.

Yes. If the honest answer is that it's fine, the report says so and the £95 is the end of it.
